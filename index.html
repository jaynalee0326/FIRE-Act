FIREFirearm Integrity & Responsible Eligibility (FIRE) Act

A focused proposal to establish a consistent federal process for covered private firearm transfers through background checks, a seven-day waiting period, privacy protections, and federal implementation.

Read the BillRead the Letter
Core proposal
7 days

A defined waiting period after the required background-check process begins, while preserving lawful ownership and privacy.

At a glance

What the FIRE Act would change

The bill is intentionally narrow and concrete so it can be debated, amended, and implemented within the House simulation.

01

Background checks

Covered private transfers would use an FFL or another federally authorized transfer point to complete the required federal check.

02

Seven-day waiting period

A covered firearm could not be physically transferred until seven calendar days have elapsed and the purchaser is legally eligible.

03

Privacy protections

The bill does not create a publicly searchable national firearm-ownership registry and limits use of background-check information.

04

Federal implementation

ATF and the FBI would issue implementation guidance, while Congress authorizes funding to support NICS capacity.

Why this issue

A specific policy, not a blanket ban

The FIRE Act focuses on the transfer process rather than prohibiting lawful firearm ownership. Its supporters can argue that a consistent process closes a gap in private transfers; opponents can question federal reach, waiting periods, cost, and implementation.

Arguments in favor

Supporters may argue that requiring a background check for covered private transfers makes eligibility rules more consistent and reduces opportunities for prohibited purchasers to receive firearms through transactions that otherwise avoid a dealer-facilitated check.

Questions for debate

Representatives may debate whether seven days is appropriate, how rural or remote transfers should work, how much implementation should cost, and whether the federal government should impose the same process nationwide.

House simulation

Built for committee debate

Possible amendment questions: Should the waiting period be shorter? Should exceptions exist for certain transfers? What funding level is appropriate? How should the bill address states with existing transfer requirements?

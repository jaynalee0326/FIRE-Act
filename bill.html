Firearm Integrity & Responsible Eligibility (FIRE) Act

Domestic Affairs · Gun Control

Purpose and Goals

Whereas firearm injuries and deaths remain a significant public-safety concern in the United States, and federal law applies different background-check requirements depending on how a firearm is transferred;

Be it enacted by the House of Representatives and the United States Senate in Congress assembled, that the purpose of this Act is to establish a consistent federal process for covered firearm transfers, prevent prohibited purchasers from lawfully receiving firearms through covered private transfers, and provide a defined waiting period between initiation of the purchase process and transfer.

Section 1: Short Title

  1. This Act may be cited as the “Firearm Integrity & Responsible Eligibility (FIRE) Act.”

Section 2: Definitions

  1. Firearm means a firearm as defined under federal law.
  2. Private transfer means a transfer between two persons who are not federally licensed firearms dealers, manufacturers, or importers, when the transfer is made by sale, trade, or other exchange of value.
  3. National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) means the federal background-check system operated by the FBI for determining whether a prospective firearm recipient is prohibited from receiving or possessing a firearm.
  4. Federal firearms licensee (FFL) means a person licensed under federal law to manufacture, import, or deal in firearms.

Section 3: Background Checks for Covered Private Transfers

  1. Before completing a covered private transfer, the seller shall have the prospective purchaser undergo a federal firearm background check through an FFL or another federally authorized transfer point.
  2. The purchaser shall provide the identification and information required for a lawful NICS check.
  3. The firearm may not be transferred if NICS or the appropriate state point of contact determines that the purchaser is prohibited from receiving or possessing firearms under federal law.
  4. Transfers already subject to a federal background-check requirement shall continue to comply with all applicable federal requirements.

Section 4: Seven-Day Waiting Period

  1. A firearm covered by this Act may not be physically transferred until seven calendar days have elapsed after the required background-check process has been initiated.
  2. A transfer may occur at the end of the seven-day period only if the purchaser is legally eligible to receive the firearm.
  3. The waiting period applies to transfers covered by this Act regardless of whether the transaction is facilitated by an FFL.
  4. The waiting period does not prevent a lawful purchaser from beginning the background-check process immediately.

Section 5: Recordkeeping and Privacy

  1. An FFL facilitating a covered private transfer shall maintain records required by federal firearms law.
  2. The federal government shall not create a publicly searchable national registry of individual firearm ownership under this Act.
  3. Information collected for a background check shall be used only for purposes authorized by federal law and protected against unauthorized disclosure.

Section 6: Federal Implementation and Funding

  1. The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), in coordination with the FBI, shall issue guidance necessary to implement this Act.
  2. The Attorney General shall establish procedures allowing private parties to complete covered transfers through participating FFLs without requiring the private seller to become an FFL solely for that transaction.
  3. Congress shall authorize such sums as are necessary to improve the capacity of NICS and support implementation of this Act.

Section 7: Enforcement

  1. A person who knowingly transfers a firearm in violation of the background-check requirement established by this Act shall be subject to penalties established under federal firearms law.
  2. A person who knowingly transfers a firearm to an individual whom the person knows is prohibited from receiving or possessing firearms shall remain subject to all applicable federal criminal penalties.
  3. Nothing in this Act limits federal or state law-enforcement authority to enforce other applicable firearms laws.

Section 8: Effective Date

  1. This Act shall take effect 180 days after enactment.
  2. The Attorney General shall publish implementing regulations and guidance before the effective date.

Conclusion

Through the passage of the Firearm Integrity & Responsible Eligibility (FIRE) Act, Congress would establish a consistent process for covered private firearm transfers while preserving lawful ownership, protecting personal information, and providing federal agencies with a defined implementation framework.
